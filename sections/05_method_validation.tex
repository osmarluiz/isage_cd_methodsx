\subsection{The Amazon session}
\label{sec:val_session}

We validated iSAGE-CD by running a working annotation session over
a scene of the Brazilian Amazon in the region of the deforestation
arc. The imagery is PlanetScope surface reflectance~\cite{nicfi2021basemaps} in four
bands (blue, green, red, near infrared) at 3\,m ground sample distance;
the scene is diced into 2000 tiles of $1024 \times 1024$ pixels.
Two class indices are used: \emph{no change} and \emph{change}
(deforestation between the two dates), consistent with the binary
supervision that operational deforestation products such as
PRODES~\cite{prodes} and MapBiomas~\cite{mapbiomas} provide at the polygon level.

The session comprises two annotation blocks, both covering the
PRODES 2022 monitoring window (Aug 2021 -- Jul 2022). Block~A uses
a Jun 2021 -- Jun 2022 image pair and holds tiles
\texttt{0000-0999}; Block~B uses the calendar-aligned Aug 2021 --
Jul 2022 pair and holds tiles \texttt{1000-1999}. The two blocks
share no tiles (a spatial offset separates them), but they
annotate the same event year with different reference-date
choices. This configuration is not a temporal-generalization
test; it reflects the operational sequence in which the session
was actually built, and the two blocks together provided the pool
from which the training iterations were carried out.

Annotation was performed by a single expert working the tool as a
practitioner would --- no budget of clicks per tile was imposed and
no target number of iterations was set in advance. In total, 1999
of the 2000 tiles received at least one point; 143{,}634 points
were placed, with a median of 43 points per tile (min 3, max 702)
and an approximately balanced class split (54.6\% \emph{no change},
45.4\% \emph{change}). Working this way, the specialist ran five
iterations of the click-train-predict loop; the convergence pattern
across those iterations is described next.

% -------------------------------------------------------------
% 5.2 Convergence across iterations
% -------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Convergence across iterations}
\label{sec:val_convergence}

Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows the training loss over all five
iterations, plotted against cumulative epoch. Each iteration is
trained until the loss plateaus (150 epochs by default, except the
short warm-start of round v2). The best per-iteration loss falls
from 0.0134 in v1 to a similar 0.0153 in v5; the intermediate v2
warm-start attained the lowest observed value (0.0086) on the
subset of tiles already annotated at that point.

\begin{figure}[!h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{figures/fig_convergence.pdf}
  \caption{Training loss across the five iterations of the Amazon
    session, on a logarithmic scale. Colours identify iterations;
    dotted vertical lines mark iteration boundaries. Each iteration
    resumes from the previous best model, but the loss visibly
    jumps up whenever new points are folded in (most clearly at
    the boundaries of v3 and v5) and is then driven back to the
    $\sim\!10^{-2}$ regime by continued training. The best-loss
    epoch of each round is highlighted.}
  \label{fig:convergence}
\end{figure}

Two features of the curve are worth noting because they reflect
how the iSAGE loop is meant to work rather than any peculiarity of
our dataset. First, the loss does not decrease monotonically across
iterations: each round adds points, and every new point that the
model was previously wrong about increases the loss transiently
before training absorbs it. The spikes at the beginnings of v3 and
v5 are the visible signature of this: v3 added the second half of
Block~A and v5 introduced Block~B. Second, the plateau value that
each iteration reaches is remarkably stable
($\approx\!10^{-2}$ across all five), which is consistent with the
iSAGE observation that a small number of error-focused clicks per
round is enough to close most of the gap to dense supervision. We
do not compare against a dense-supervision baseline here; a
rigorous quantitative evaluation is the subject of a separate
manuscript in preparation.

\subsection{Qualitative examples}
\label{sec:val_qualitative}

Beyond the loss curve, the most direct check of the method is
whether the resulting per-tile predictions are of usable quality.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows four representative tiles from
Block~B (Aug 2021 -- Jul 2022 pair). Each row contains, from left
to right, the before image, the after image, the sparse
annotations placed by the expert, and the v5 prediction overlaid
on the after image. The tiles were chosen to span the recurring
patterns of the arc of deforestation: a large consolidated
clearing, a fragmented clearing with edges and remnant vegetation
islands, an intrusion along an unpaved road, and a control tile
dominated by standing forest without recent change.

\begin{figure}[!h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_qualitative.pdf}
  \caption{Four representative tiles from Block~B. Columns:
    PlanetScope T1 (Aug 2021), PlanetScope T2 (Jul 2022), sparse
    annotations placed via iSAGE-CD (red circles $=$ change, green
    circles $=$ no change), and the v5 model prediction on T2
    (translucent red overlay $=$ predicted change). Rows: a
    consolidated clearing (tile 1001, 86.5\% PRODES change), a
    fragmented clearing with remnant vegetation islands (tile
    1010, 52\%), an edge/small-clearing case near a river (tile
    1064, 18.6\%), and a control tile of standing forest with only
    pre-existing clearings persisting between the two dates (tile
    1889, 0.2\%).}
  \label{fig:qualitative}
\end{figure}

The four cases are qualitatively coherent with the input: the
predicted change footprint tracks the observable T1-to-T2
difference; edges of large clearings are traced without leaking
into standing forest; the fragmented case retains the vegetation
islands rather than filling them. Visual inspection is not a
substitute for a quantitative comparison with dense supervision or
with PRODES polygons, and we do not claim otherwise here. What the
qualitative panel does establish is that a working session run
through the iSAGE-CD tool, with the protocol described in
Section~\ref{sec:overview} onward, produces per-tile predictions
that a domain expert can inspect and act upon --- the operational
output that the method was designed to deliver.
