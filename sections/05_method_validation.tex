% 5.1 The Amazon session

\subsection{The Amazon session}
\label{sec:val_session}

We validated iSAGE-CD by running a working annotation session over
a scene of the Brazilian Amazon in the region of the deforestation
arc. The imagery is PlanetScope surface reflectance~\cite{nicfi2021basemaps} in four
bands (blue, green, red, near infrared) at 3\,m ground sample
distance, and the scene is diced into 2000 tiles of
$1024 \times 1024$ pixels.
Two class indices are used: \emph{no change} and \emph{change}
(deforestation between the two dates), consistent with the binary
supervision that operational deforestation products such as
PRODES~\cite{prodes} and MapBiomas~\cite{mapbiomas} provide at the polygon level.

The session comprises two annotation blocks, both covering the
PRODES 2022 monitoring window (Aug 2021 -- Jul 2022). Block~A uses
a Jun 2021 -- Jun 2022 image pair and holds tiles
\texttt{0000-0999}, and Block~B uses the calendar-aligned Aug 2021
-- Jul 2022 pair and holds tiles \texttt{1000-1999}. The two
blocks share no tiles (a spatial offset separates them), but they
annotate the same event year with different reference-date
choices. This configuration is not a temporal-generalization
test. It reflects the operational sequence in which the session
was actually built, and the two blocks together provided the pool
from which the training iterations were carried out.

% -------------------------------------------------------------
% 5.2 Running the loop
% -------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Running the loop}
\label{sec:val_convergence}

Annotation was performed by a single expert working the tool as a
practitioner would: no budget of clicks per tile was imposed, no
target number of iterations was set in advance, and no validation
or test tiles were held out. The session was a production
annotation effort rather than a controlled experiment, so every
labeled tile feeds training, which is how the loop runs in real
use. In total, 1999 of the 2000 tiles received at least one point
and 143{,}634 clicks were placed, with a median of 43 points per
tile (min 3, max 702) and an approximately balanced class split
(54.6\% \emph{no change}, 45.4\% \emph{change}). These points
cover 0.007\% of the roughly $2.1\times10^{9}$ pixels in the
session, about one labeled pixel in every 14{,}600. Working this
way, the expert ran five iterations of the click-train-predict
loop.

Figure~\ref{fig:pointsperiter} shows how that effort was spent
across the iterations. The cold-start pass of v1 carried the bulk
of the clicks, 54{,}913 points on Block~A. Rounds v2 and v4 were
short refinement passes that added only 205 and 131 points. Round
v3 extended the annotation to the second half of Block~A and
nearly doubled the pool with 38{,}318 further points, and v5
introduced Block~B with another 49{,}925 on a previously unseen
tile set. The pool available to the last model is 143{,}492
distinct labeled pixels, 65{,}170 of them \emph{change} and
78{,}322 \emph{no change}, so the supervision stays close to
balanced even though change covers a small fraction of the scene.

\begin{figure}[!h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_points_per_iter.pdf}
  \caption{Annotation effort across the five iterations of the
    Amazon session. (a)~Points added at each round, stacked by
    class (red $=$ \emph{change}, green $=$ \emph{no change}), with
    the round total above each bar. (b)~Cumulative pool available
    for training at the end of each round.}
  \label{fig:pointsperiter}
\end{figure}

Training instantiated the reference protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:training}: a U-Net with an EfficientNet-B7
encoder over eight-channel stacks of the two dates, the
error-weighted loss of iSAGE, and 150 epochs per round with a warm
start from the previous best model. Full-image predictions used a
sliding window of 1024 pixels with 50\% overlap. Each of those is
a choice of this session, not a constant of the software: the tool
sees dated images, a class definition, and pairs, whatever the
sensor, the theme, or the model.

The rounds are visible on the predictions themselves.
Figure~\ref{fig:iterprogression} shows four Block~A tiles under
the successive models v1 to v5, and what changes between columns
tracks the effort of Figure~\ref{fig:pointsperiter}. Rounds v2 and
v4 leave the footprint almost untouched, which is what a couple of
hundred added points can do, while v3 and v5 redraw it. In the
consolidated clearing of the second row, v1 covers standing forest
at the top of the tile and the later rounds pull the boundary back
onto the cleared area. We do not compare against a
dense-supervision baseline here, and a rigorous quantitative
evaluation is the subject of a separate manuscript in preparation.

\begin{figure}[!h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_iteration_progression.pdf}
  \caption{The same tiles seen by the model of every round. Columns:
    PlanetScope T1 (before), PlanetScope T2 (after), and the
    predicted change footprint of models v1 to v5 overlaid on T2
    (translucent red). Rows are four Block~A tiles spanning change
    fractions from 0.8\% to 69.1\%, given as the PRODES change
    percentage of the tile. The columns that move are v3 and v5,
    the rounds that added most of the points.}
  \label{fig:iterprogression}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Qualitative inspection}
\label{sec:val_qualitative}

The most direct check of the method is whether the resulting
per-tile predictions are of usable quality.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows four representative tiles from
Block~B (Aug 2021 -- Jul 2022 pair). Each row contains, from left
to right, the before image, the after image, the sparse
annotations placed by the expert, and the v5 prediction overlaid
on the after image. The tiles were chosen to span the recurring
patterns of the arc of deforestation: a large consolidated
clearing, a fragmented clearing with edges and remnant vegetation
islands, an intrusion along an unpaved road, and a control tile
dominated by standing forest without recent change.

\begin{figure}[!h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_qualitative.pdf}
  \caption{Four representative tiles from Block~B. Columns:
    PlanetScope T1 (Aug 2021), PlanetScope T2 (Jul 2022), sparse
    annotations placed via iSAGE-CD (red circles $=$ change, green
    circles $=$ no change), and the v5 model prediction on T2
    (translucent red overlay $=$ predicted change). Rows: a
    consolidated clearing (tile 1001, 86.5\% PRODES change), a
    fragmented clearing with remnant vegetation islands (tile
    1010, 52\%), an edge/small-clearing case near a river (tile
    1064, 18.6\%), and a control tile of standing forest with only
    pre-existing clearings persisting between the two dates (tile
    1889, 0.2\%).}
  \label{fig:qualitative}
\end{figure}

The four cases are qualitatively coherent with the input: the
predicted change footprint tracks the observable T1-to-T2
difference, edges of large clearings are traced without leaking
into standing forest, and the fragmented case retains the
vegetation islands rather than filling them. This inspection is
also how the session ended. There is no held-out metric to
optimize, and the loop stops when the expert, reviewing the
predictions of the current round, is satisfied that the dataset
supports its intended use. That point was reached at v5. Visual
inspection is not a substitute for a quantitative comparison with
dense supervision or with PRODES polygons, and we do not claim
otherwise here. What the qualitative panel does establish is that
a working session run through the iSAGE-CD tool, with the protocol
described in Section~\ref{sec:overview} onward, produces per-tile
predictions that a domain expert can inspect and accept, and that
acceptance is the operational output the method is designed to
deliver.
