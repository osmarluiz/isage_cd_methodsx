The session reported here covers a single scene, a single sensor,
and one event window with two classes, so nothing in this article
shows how the protocol behaves on other sensors or a larger class
set. The label format also fixes the annotation unit at two dates,
so a model that consumes a longer series at once falls outside it,
even though a scene may hold many dates.

The validation is qualitative and rests on one expert, with no
dense-supervision baseline trained and no tiles held out, so the
article establishes that the protocol produces output an expert
accepts rather than that the output is accurate. A quantitative
comparison against dense supervision and PRODES polygons is the
subject of a separate manuscript in preparation.

The annotator is a local PyQt5 desktop application, was tested
mostly on Windows, and reads PNG in pixel coordinates, so imagery
in other formats has to be converted and any georeferencing of the
output is done downstream. It ships without a trainer, so adopting
it for a new task means supplying training and inference scripts
that honour the folder contract of
Table~\ref{tab:iteration_folder}, with the reference
implementation of iSAGE as the shortest path to that.
