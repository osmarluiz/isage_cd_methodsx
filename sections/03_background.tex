Change detection between two co-registered dates underpins deforestation
monitoring, disaster response, and land-use accounting~\cite{prodes,mapbiomas}.
Its supervised formulation requires pixel-level labels on both dates, a
cost that grows with area, event frequency, and the precision demanded by
downstream reporting. Dense annotation of a single 1000-tile Amazon scene
can consume weeks of expert time; extending the protocol across years or
biomes becomes the practical bottleneck.

Recent work on sparse point supervision has challenged the assumption
that dense labels are unavoidable. iSAGE~\cite{isage} showed that, for
remote-sensing semantic segmentation, expert clicks targeting confident
model errors recover the quality of dense supervision at a fraction of a
percent of the pixel budget: 97.2\% of the dense mIoU on BsB Aerial with
0.040\% of pixels, and a match of the dense baseline on ISPRS Vaihingen
(76.78\% vs.\ 76.65\% mIoU) with only 0.011\% of pixels. The key mechanism
is architectural minimalism -- an error-weighted loss amplifies the
gradient at each click, and no auxiliary machinery (pseudo-labels,
conditional random fields, foundation-model prompts) is required. The
annotation record itself functions as an auditable, extensible dataset.

iSAGE, however, addresses single-image segmentation. Change detection
imposes constraints that a mono-temporal design does not accommodate.
First, the semantics of a label depend on \emph{two} dates: the same
polygon may indicate ``recent deforestation'' relative to one reference
image and ``stable deforestation'' relative to another. Second, the same
fixed scene is often labeled against multiple candidate references (for
example, a monthly series in which the annotator picks the cleanest one),
and each such comparison is an independent training sample. Third, storing
labels alongside a single image forces either duplication or
projection~\cite{isprsCDreview}, both of which risk introducing subtle
inconsistencies when the reference changes.

iSAGE-CD extends the iSAGE philosophy to the bi-temporal setting through
three design decisions. (i)~\emph{Pair-anchored labels}: each JSON file
records a single (reference, frame) pair and carries only points that are
meaningful under that pair; changing the reference switches the point
set. (ii)~\emph{Dual synchronized canvases}: zoom, pan, and points are
co-registered on both dates, and the click gesture is agnostic to which
canvas the user acts on. (iii)~\emph{Session-as-dataset}: the annotation
tree mirrors the dataset layout, so the annotation record \emph{is} the
training set and leakage-free splits reduce to splitting by frame folder.
The iterative click-loop of iSAGE is preserved: after each training
round, the model's confident errors on the current frame guide the next
set of clicks; no expansion machinery is inserted between click and loss.

This article describes iSAGE-CD as a reproducible method. Complete
quantitative evaluation against dense supervision, comparison with
alternative sparse-annotation tools, and downstream deforestation
mapping are the subject of a separate manuscript. Here we focus on the
tool, the session format, and the protocol, and validate them on an
Amazon deforestation session aligned to the PRODES 2022 calendar.
