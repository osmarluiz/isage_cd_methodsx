Change detection compares two co-registered satellite images and
tells you which pixels changed. Deforestation monitoring, disaster
response, and land-use mapping all depend on it. Supervised methods
need per-pixel labels for both dates. Producing those labels is slow:
a single 1000-tile Amazon scene can take several weeks of an expert's
time. Scaling to more years or more regions is the practical
bottleneck.

iSAGE~\cite{isage} showed that dense labels are not always necessary.
For remote-sensing semantic segmentation, an expert who clicks only
on the model's confident mistakes reaches nearly the same quality as
full dense annotation. On the BsB Aerial dataset, iSAGE recovered
97.2\% of the dense mIoU using 0.040\% of the pixels. On ISPRS
Vaihingen it matched the dense baseline (76.78\% versus 76.65\% mIoU)
with 0.011\% of the pixels. The mechanism is small: an error-weighted
loss amplifies the gradient at each click. There are no
pseudo-labels, no conditional random fields, no foundation-model
prompts. The annotation record is the dataset.

iSAGE works on one image. Change detection asks more. A label only
has meaning when both dates are present: the same polygon can
indicate ``recent deforestation'' against one before-image and
``old deforestation'' against another. The same after-image is often
labeled against several before-images, and each pairing is a separate
training sample. Standard annotation tools attach labels to one
image. If the paired image changes, the labels have to be copied or
reprojected, and both routes introduce silent errors.

iSAGE-CD adapts iSAGE to change detection. Three design choices
differ from the original. First, labels are anchored to a pair, not
to an image: each JSON file stores one (reference, frame) pair and
holds only the points that make sense in that pair. Second, the
annotator shows two synchronized canvases; zoom, pan, and clicks are
shared between them. Third, the annotation tree and the dataset are
the same directory, so splitting by frame folder gives a
leakage-free split by construction. The iterative click loop, the
error-weighted loss, and the refusal to insert machinery between
click and gradient are inherited from iSAGE unchanged.

This article describes iSAGE-CD as a reproducible method. Full
quantitative evaluation against dense supervision, comparison with
PRODES, and a study with multiple human annotators are the subject
of a separate manuscript in preparation. Here we cover the tool, the
session format, and the protocol, and we validate them on an Amazon
deforestation session using Planet imagery aligned to the PRODES
2022 calendar (August 2021 -- July 2022).
