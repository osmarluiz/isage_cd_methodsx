iSAGE~\cite{isage} recently showed that sparse point supervision can
match dense labeling in remote-sensing semantic segmentation. Expert
clicks placed on the model's confident mistakes recover most of the
mIoU obtained with full dense labels. On the BsB Aerial dataset,
iSAGE reached 97.2\% of the dense mIoU with 0.040\% of the pixels.
On ISPRS Vaihingen, it matched the dense baseline (76.78\% versus
76.65\% mIoU) with only 0.011\% of the pixels. The mechanism is
minimal: an error-weighted loss amplifies the gradient at each click.
There are no pseudo-labels, no conditional random fields, no
foundation-model prompts. The annotation record is the dataset.

Change detection is a different task, and one in which annotation is
at least as demanding. The output is not the class of a pixel in a
single image but the presence of change across two co-registered
dates. The labeler has to compare both images and reason about the
transition, not about one scene. Producing dense per-pixel change
masks over large areas remains costly.

The iSAGE approach has not been extended to change detection. Its
original design targets single-image semantic segmentation, and a
label is bound to one image. In the bi-temporal case, a label is
anchored to a pair of dates, and the same fixed scene can be labeled
against several reference images. Bringing the iSAGE philosophy to
change detection is the goal of this work.

iSAGE-CD adapts iSAGE to the bi-temporal setting. It preserves the
iterative click loop, the error-weighted loss, and the refusal to
insert machinery between click and gradient. Three design choices
distinguish it from the original. First, labels are anchored to a
pair, not to an image: each JSON file stores one (reference, frame)
pair and holds only the points that make sense in that pair. Second,
the annotator shows two synchronized canvases; zoom, pan, and clicks
are shared between them. Third, the annotation tree and the dataset
are the same directory, and splitting by frame folder is a
leakage-free split by construction.

This article describes iSAGE-CD as a reproducible method. Full
quantitative evaluation against dense supervision, comparison with
PRODES, and a study with multiple human annotators are the subject
of a separate manuscript in preparation. Here we cover the tool, the
session format, and the protocol, and validate them on an Amazon
deforestation session using Planet imagery aligned to the PRODES
2022 calendar (August 2021 -- July 2022).
